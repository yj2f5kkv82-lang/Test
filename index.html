Origins

The term Intellectual Dark Web (IDW) was coined by mathematician and entrepreneur Eric Weinstein around 2017 to describe a loose network of public intellectuals, podcasters, comedians, and academics who felt increasingly unable to express heterodox views within mainstream institutions — universities, legacy media, and political parties.

The IDW emerged in the context of a cultural moment defined by several intersecting forces: the rise of long-form podcast culture (particularly The Joe Rogan Experience), growing concerns about campus free-speech controversies, and a widespread sense among certain intellectuals that political correctness and ideological conformity were suppressing open inquiry.

Rather than a formal organisation, the IDW was a brand — a rhetorical umbrella for thinkers who shared a commitment to "forbidden conversations" and who had largely built independent audiences outside traditional gatekeepers via YouTube, Patreon, podcasts, and Substack.

Key Figures

🎙️

Joe Rogan

Comedian and host of The Joe Rogan Experience. His long-form interview format gave IDW figures a massive mainstream platform, reaching tens of millions of listeners per episode.

🧬

Sam Harris

Neuroscientist, philosopher, and author. Harris used his Making Sense podcast to explore consciousness, religion, free will, and political tribalism from a secular rationalist perspective.

📖

Jordan Peterson

Canadian clinical psychologist and professor. His opposition to Canadian Bill C-16 and the subsequent viral lectures on mythology, responsibility, and identity propelled him to global fame.

🔬

Eric Weinstein

Managing director of Thiel Capital and mathematician who coined the IDW term. He argued that establishment institutions had become hostile to genuine intellectual risk-taking.

🧪

Bret Weinstein

Evolutionary biologist and Eric's brother. His stand during the 2017 Evergreen State College controversy — where he refused to leave campus on a "Day of Absence" — made him a focal point of campus free-speech debates.

✍️

Bari Weiss

Journalist who wrote the defining New York Times profile of the IDW in May 2018, bringing the term into widespread public consciousness. She later left the Times and founded The Free Press.

🎤

Dave Rubin

Political commentator and host of The Rubin Report. A former progressive who moved toward classical liberalism, he interviewed many IDW figures and helped cross-pollinate their audiences.

🏋️

Ben Shapiro

Conservative commentator, author, and founder of The Daily Wire. Though ideologically distinct from some IDW figures, he was included in many early discussions of the network.

🔭

Heather Heying

Evolutionary biologist and Bret Weinstein's wife. She was also pushed out of Evergreen State and has co-hosted the DarkHorse Podcast, exploring evolutionary biology and culture.

📻

Michael Shermer

Science writer, historian, and founder of Skeptic magazine. A longtime advocate for scientific skepticism and rational inquiry.

Core Ideas

The IDW was never a coherent ideological movement — its members disagreed sharply on politics, religion, and policy. What united them was more epistemological than political:

Free Speech & Open Inquiry

A shared commitment to the idea that no topic should be off-limits for rational discussion, and that social pressure to silence inquiry is itself a threat to truth-seeking.

Anti-Tribalism

Criticism of both progressive and conservative identity politics, and a belief that tribal loyalty distorts reasoning and crowds out nuance.

Institutional Distrust

Skepticism toward universities, mainstream media, and political parties as reliable arbiters of truth — paired with enthusiasm for decentralised media platforms.

Empiricism & Science

A declared commitment to following evidence wherever it leads, even into socially or politically uncomfortable territory.

Long-Form Dialogue

A rejection of sound-bite media culture in favour of hours-long podcast conversations as a vehicle for genuine intellectual exchange.

Individual Responsibility

Particularly emphasised by Jordan Peterson — the idea that personal meaning and social improvement begin with individual accountability, not systemic change alone.

Timeline

2012–2015

The Podcast Era Begins

Joe Rogan's podcast grows into one of the most downloaded shows in the world. Sam Harris launches Waking Up (later Making Sense). The infrastructure for long-form independent media matures.

2016

Jordan Peterson Goes Viral

University of Toronto professor Jordan Peterson posts a YouTube series criticising Bill C-16, a Canadian bill adding gender identity to protected human rights categories. His refusal to use compelled pronouns sparks national controversy and a massive online following.

April 2017

The Evergreen State Controversy

Biology professor Bret Weinstein objects to a "Day of Absence" at Evergreen State College that asked white students and staff to leave campus. He is subsequently hounded by student protesters and eventually resigns with a settlement, becoming a cause célèbre for campus free-speech advocates.

2017

Eric Weinstein Coins "IDW"

Eric Weinstein uses the term "Intellectual Dark Web" to describe the emerging network of heterodox thinkers building audiences outside mainstream institutions. The phrase captures something in the cultural air.

January 2018

12 Rules for Life Published

Jordan Peterson's self-help book becomes a global bestseller, spending months at the top of charts in multiple countries. Peterson embarks on a sold-out world tour, cementing his status as the IDW's most prominent public figure.

May 2018

The New York Times Profile

Bari Weiss publishes "Meet the Renegades of the Intellectual Dark Web" in The New York Times, complete with a stylised group photograph. The article crystallises the IDW as a recognisable cultural phenomenon and sparks intense debate about its significance.

2018–2019

Peak Influence

IDW figures dominate podcast charts, YouTube algorithms, and cultural conversation. Patreon accounts and speaking tours generate substantial independent revenue. The "Intellectual Dark Web" label is debated, embraced, and mocked across the political spectrum.

2019–2021

Fragmentation & Internal Tensions

Members diverge on COVID-19, vaccines, and the 2020 US election. Sam Harris and Jordan Peterson clash repeatedly. Some figures drift toward more explicitly partisan positions. Critics argue the IDW has drifted from its stated commitment to empiricism and nuance.

2020

Bari Weiss Leaves The New York Times

Weiss resigns from the Times, citing a hostile internal culture and self-censorship. Her resignation letter goes viral. She later founds The Free Press, an independent media outlet aligned with IDW values.

2021–Present

Legacy & Diffusion

The IDW label fades but its influence persists. Independent media, Substack journalism, and long-form podcasting are now mainstream. Many IDW figures continue to command large audiences. Debates about free speech, institutional trust, and heterodox thought that the IDW amplified remain central to public discourse.

Legacy & Criticism

Contributions

  • Helped normalise long-form, nuanced public conversation outside mainstream media constraints.
  • Demonstrated that independent creators could build sustainable audiences without institutional backing.
  • Kept debates about free speech, epistemic rigour, and institutional accountability in the public eye.
  • Influenced the growth of Substack, independent podcasting, and the broader "creator economy" of ideas.
  • Provided a forum for heterodox perspectives on psychology, evolutionary biology, and political theory.

Criticisms

  • Critics argued that "forbidden ideas" were often simply right-wing ideas repackaged as brave heterodoxy.
  • The claim to pure empiricism was challenged by selective engagement with scientific consensus (e.g., on race and IQ).
  • Many figures drifted toward partisan positions that contradicted their stated anti-tribalism.
  • The network remained predominantly white, male, and Western, limiting its range of "heterodox" perspectives.
  • Commercial incentives of the attention economy may have rewarded provocation over genuine intellectual rigour.

Regardless of where one stands on its figures and ideas, the Intellectual Dark Web represented a genuine cultural moment: the collision of digital media, institutional crisis, and the perennial human appetite for ideas that feel transgressive, honest, and unafraid.
